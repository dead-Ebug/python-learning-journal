## What is Inside `hello.py`?
`hello.py` is your very first Python script. Its only job is to talk to the computer and tell it to show messages on your screen using the `print()` function.

---

## Core Breakdown of Concepts

### 1. The `print()` Function
* **What it is:** A built-in command built straight into Python.
* **Analogy:** Think of it like an intercom system. You feed a message into it, and it broadcasts that message onto your computer screen.

### 2. Parentheses `()`
* **What they do:** They act like a container or a pair of hands holding the information you want to give to `print`.
* **Analogy:** If `print` is a delivery driver, the parentheses are the delivery box holding the item. Without the box container, the driver has nothing to carry.

### 3. Quotation Marks `""`
* **What they do:** They wrap around your text to tell Python, *"Hey, treat this exact wording as plain English text (a string), not as a secret computer command or a math problem."*
* **Analogy:** Wrapping text in quotes is like putting it inside a clear display case. People can read what's inside, but the computer won't try to open or execute it.

### 4. Comments (`#`)
* **What they do:** Any line starting with a hashtag is a sticky note for humans. Python completely skips and ignores them when running the code.
